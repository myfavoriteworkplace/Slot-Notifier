Good — now I can see the full booking flow. Let me give you a thorough UX analysis before touching anything.
What's working well:
The two-step progress indicator (Your Details → Pick a Slot) is the right architecture. The dark green header with the date and clinic name is a strong contextual anchor — the patient always knows where they are and when. The "Email verified ✓" floating badge is a smart trust signal. The accordion pattern for "What brings you in?" is the right approach for a multi-select symptom picker. The sub-symptom chips (Sensitivity to hot/cold, Sharp pain, etc.) are genuinely useful for the clinic to triage — this is sophisticated clinical UX that most booking tools don't have.
The problems — full analysis:
Problem 1 — The form feels like a form, not a conversation.
The current layout reads as a traditional data entry form: FULL NAME → AGE & GENDER → PHONE NUMBER → EMAIL ADDRESS → WHAT BRINGS YOU IN. This is functional but cold. A patient booking a dental appointment is often anxious. The form's uppercase label style and sequential field layout feels like filling in a government document. The best dental booking flows (Practo, Zocdoc, Clove Dental's newer UI) have moved to a conversational pattern — one question at a time, friendly language, progress that feels like a chat rather than a bureaucratic checklist.
Problem 2 — The email verification flow creates visual confusion.
There are currently two "Email verified" elements on screen simultaneously — a floating green pill badge in the top right corner AND a green confirmation card in the middle of the form. The email field itself has a yellow highlight. This is three different visual treatments for the same status. A user's eye doesn't know where to look. The yellow email field background in particular is jarring — it reads as a warning or error colour in most design systems. The verification state should be communicated once, cleanly, in one place.
Problem 3 — Age & Gender feels intrusive at this stage.
The age field with spinner arrows and the gender toggles (Male/Female/Other) are placed as the second field — before phone and email. For a dental booking, age and gender are useful for the clinic but not essential to confirm a slot. Placing them so early, before the patient has even been verified, creates unnecessary friction. These belong in an optional "help us know you better" section after the primary booking is confirmed, or at minimum after phone/email are captured.
Problem 4 — The "What Brings You In?" section needs better empty state guidance.
When the accordion is collapsed (Image 2), the user sees 8 categories with emojis and chevrons. The instruction "Select at least one reason for your visit to continue" only appears at the very bottom, below the CTA button — which the patient may not scroll to see. The validation message should be inline, near the section header, not buried below the primary action. The "3 selected" counter in Image 3 is good — but it only appears after selection, not as a guide before.
Problem 5 — The symptom sub-chips are not visually prominent enough.
In Image 3, the expanded "Tooth Pain or Sensitivity" shows sub-options as pill chips. The selected ones (green tinted) vs unselected ones (white/grey border) are clear enough — but the chips are quite small and the touch targets on mobile are borderline. On a phone, tapping "Pain while chewing" accurately requires precision. The minimum comfortable tap target is 44px height — these look closer to 36px.
Problem 6 — The CTA button state is inconsistently styled.
In Image 2 (nothing selected), "View Available Slots →" appears in a muted/washed-out green — deliberately indicating disabled state. In Image 3 (items selected), it's a richer, saturated green. This is the right pattern but the disabled state looks too similar to the enabled state — it needs stronger visual differentiation. Add a lock icon or "Select a reason to continue" text inside the button when disabled, not just below it.
Problem 7 — No indication of how long this takes.
The patient has no idea if they're 20% or 80% done. The two-step indicator shows step 1 and step 2 but doesn't communicate that step 1 has multiple sub-steps (details + symptoms + notes). A micro-copy line like "Takes about 90 seconds" near the top would reduce abandonment significantly — this is one of the highest-impact changes you can make.
Problem 8 — Desktop experience is not considered.
All three screenshots are mobile. On desktop, this single-column narrow form will either be very narrow (bad) or stretch awkwardly to fill the viewport (worse). The booking flow needs a considered desktop layout — either a centred card with max-width 480px, or a split layout with the clinic/appointment context on the left and the form on the right. Your existing landing page already uses the split layout pattern effectively.
My UX recommendations — prioritised:
Change 1 — Conversational tone in labels. Replace uppercase technical labels with friendly questions. "FULL NAME" → "What's your name?". "PHONE NUMBER" → "Best number to reach you?". "WHAT BRINGS YOU IN?" → "What's bothering you today?". This single change dramatically shifts the emotional register from bureaucratic to caring.
Change 2 — Consolidate email verification to one signal. Remove the floating badge. Keep only the inline green confirmation card. The email field returns to normal styling once verified — no yellow background. Add a small green dot/checkmark inside the email field's right edge as the only persistent indicator.
Change 3 — Move Age & Gender to below the symptoms section, clearly labelled as optional with a reason ("Helps your doctor prepare for your visit"). Make it collapsible — most patients will skip it, and that's fine.
Change 4 — Add inline validation guidance for symptoms. Put "Choose at least one — this helps us assign the right specialist" as a subtitle directly under the section header, visible without scrolling. Remove the instruction from below the CTA.
Change 5 — Increase symptom chip tap targets to minimum 44px height. Slightly increase font size from what appears to be 13px to 14px.
Change 6 — Differentiate the disabled CTA more strongly. When no symptoms are selected: grey background, lock icon, "Select a reason first". When one or more selected: full green, arrow icon, "View Available Slots →".
Change 7 — Add time estimate. Between the header and the first field, add: "Quick booking · 2 steps · about 90 seconds". Small, muted, but high-impact for reducing drop-off.
Change 8 — Desktop layout. Centred card, max-width 480px, 24px card padding, white card on light grey background. The clinic header becomes a compact strip at the top of the card rather than a full-width dark band.
The one change that will have the most impact:
Consolidating the email verification to a single visual signal and removing the yellow email field. Right now when a user's email is verified, the screen shows three simultaneous "verified" treatments, which paradoxically makes the state feel uncertain rather than confirmed. One clear signal, done well, is far more reassuring than three competing ones.
